A steering vector from incorrect vs correct rollouts beats the published weight edit

Qwen3.5-0.8B loops on most math problems. On the same 60 held-out MATH-500 problems, adding one difference-of-means vector after layer 15 gets 31 right; the published 64-neuron weight edit gets 20; the unedited model gets 7. The vector was built without knowing the failure was looping.

Limits. The post's protocol uses a 32,768-token cap and four samples per problem; the shorter cap favours whichever method finishes sooner. The 60 problems skew toward short statements. The vector rests on 15 correct rollouts.

All interventions on 36 practice problems

MATH train practice problems, one sample each, 8,192-token cap. Directions built from correctness or from hitting the token limit both remove loops and reach answers. Directions built from loop labels remove loops but rarely reach an answer. Random directions and random neurons are the controls. With 36 problems, gaps of a few problems are noise.

Can activation differences find the edited neurons?

Number of the 64 edited neurons that appear in each ranking's top 64 (3,584 neurons per layer). Activation difference alone finds 28 and 18. The edited neurons are high-variance, low-index neurons, so simple baselines already find 9 to 20.
